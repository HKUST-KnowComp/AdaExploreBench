Your job is to compare against different fields and author executable cross-field feasibility constraints according to their definition.

First identify fields connected by a strong definitional, accounting, population-containment, or physical-capacity relationship. Emit a constraint only when their jointly observed numeric values must satisfy that relation, the constraint should be some hard restriction to avoid value combinations that is obviously impossible in real world. For instance, the combination "total_number_of_objects = 30" and "number_of_type_A_object = 40" may be impossible if type A object belongs to total objects, a union cannot be less than/smaller than its part. Ordinary correlation, causal expectation, common co-movement, and typical behavior are not invariants; an empty list is valid.

Evaluate a constraint only over records where every field it references has a numeric value. Only consider cross field relations and constraints, do not encode availability, nullness, missingness, benign non-applicability, or single-field legal ranges in the expression, and never use a field's ordinary range bound as a guard for a cross-field relation. Prefer one direct comparison whenever it states the invariant. When a genuine conditional invariant is needed, use `implies` explicitly instead of using an equivalent expression of `or`.

Express each invariant with the finite structured expression grammar. A numeric atom is {"field": FIELD_ID}. A numeric term is an atom, {"const": NUMBER}, or one arithmetic node over atoms; `add` and `mul` take two to eight atoms, while `sub` and `div` take exactly two. A numeric expression is a term or one arithmetic node over terms with the same arities. A comparison uses `le|lt|ge|gt|eq` with numeric-expression `left` and `right`. A Boolean clause is a comparison, `not` of one comparison, or `and|or` over two to eight comparisons. A complete Boolean expression is a clause, `not` of one clause, `and|or` over two to eight clauses, or `implies` whose `if` and `then` are clauses. Do not nest beyond these layers. Split independent conjuncts into separate constraints instead of nesting repeated `and` nodes. All arithmetic and comparisons must be dimensionally valid. Operands of `add` and `sub` must have compatible dimensions, and the two sides of every comparison must have compatible dimensions after any explicit exact unit conversion. `mul` and `div` may be used only when their resulting dimensions make the comparison valid. Never directly compare a count with a rate or duration.

A constraint can be set among more than 2 fields, for each candidate, verify only the actual definitions to establish the constraint, do not judge merely from the field name. If any valid joint numeric assignment can violate the proposed relation, omit it. Notice that independent constraints should be established separately, thus long constraints are used only when necessary, do not combine several short constraints into a whole long expression.

Before returning, compare all retained constraints. If a constraint contains could be devide into two or more independent constraints, then split them. If two constraints impose the same restriction after commutative reordering or algebraic simplification, emit only the clearer, shallower one. Do not treat partial overlap or one-way implication as duplication.

Give each retained constraint a stable id and one concise description stating why the numeric restriction is necessary. Every constraint must have a non-empty ID that is unique within feasibility_constraints. Never reuse an ID for two constraints, even when their descriptions or expressions differ. Before returning, confirm that every field reference is one supplied ID and evaluate the exact AST on its boundary cases to ensure it states the same restriction as the description. Similar names alone do not establish equality, complementarity, containment, or interchangeability.
